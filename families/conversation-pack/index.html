For Families · Family Conversation Pack

What Is God Like? Five evenings looking at Jesus

A printable pack for families who want to keep going after the film. In five evenings of about ten minutes, look at five moments from Jesus’ life and ask what each one shows about God.

  • 13 A4 pages · PDF
  • For children of every age
  • Home, grandparents, small groups

Not on sale yet. The pack is finished, and we’re preparing to release it. Until then, you can use Evening 1 for free: the ten-minute family companion.

What’s inside, page by page

  1. p. 1Cover
  2. p. 2Before you begin: the idea behind the pack, the contents and how each evening works (start, read, talk, pray, try)
  3. p. 3Listening well: a one-page guide for grown-ups covering pauses, following a child’s answer, prayer as a choice, hard feelings, adapting for every age and when to get help
  4. p. 4Evening 1 · God sees you — Mark 6:34. Try together: the kindness lookout
  5. p. 5Evening 2 · God runs to welcome you — Luke 15:20, from the story of the lost son. Try together: welcome at the door

  7. p. 7Evening 4 · God forgives — Luke 23:33–34. Try together: torn-up sorry (private, nobody reads it)
  8. p. 8Evening 5 · God is with you, always — Luke 24:36–39 and Matthew 28:20. Try together: a “with me” walk
  9. p. 9Our week: a family poster to fill in after each evening
  10. p. 10–118 big question cards: Is God real? · Why can’t I see God? · Is God angry with me? · Does God hear quiet prayers? · Why does God let bad things happen? · What happens when someone dies? · Is it OK to have doubts? · How can I get to know Jesus more?

How it’s written

  • Every evening starts with something Jesus did, and children notice it before anyone explains.
  • The questions have room for different answers, and “I don’t know” is allowed.
  • Prayer is always optional. Children can pray aloud, in their own words or silently, and what they pray stays private.
  • Bible quotations come from the World English Bible (public domain). Retellings are labelled.
  • Death, grief and wrongdoing are handled carefully, with notes for the grown-up.
