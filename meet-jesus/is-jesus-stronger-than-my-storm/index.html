Meet Jesus · Reading 11 of 12

Is Jesus stronger than my storm?

  • Mark 4:35–41
  • About 3 minutes aloud
  • For children of every age

Read this with a grown-up, or read it yourself. When you see a pause, stop and think for a moment.

A moment you might knowWind at the window

Some nights the wind howls and the rain hammers the window. Some days feel stormy inside us too: a fight at home, a worry that won’t go away, a big change.

Pause. Has there been a “storm” in your life lately?

From the BibleAsleep in the boat

Retelling

One evening, Jesus and his friends set off in a fishing boat across a big lake. Jesus was tired, so he lay down at the back of the boat and fell asleep.

Bible text

37A big wind storm arose, and the waves beat into the boat, so much that the boat was already filled. 38He himself was in the stern, asleep on the cushion, and they woke him up, and told him, “Teacher, don’t you care that we are dying?” 39He awoke, and rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, “Peace! Be still!” The wind ceased, and there was a great calm. 40He said to them, “Why are you so afraid? How is it that you have no faith?” 41They were greatly afraid, and said to one another, “Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?”

Mark 4:37–41, World English Bible. The stern is the back of the boat. Rebuked means spoke sternly to.

Wonder together“Who then is this?”

The friends asked, “Don’t you care?” What did Jesus do next?

Pause. Read verse 39 again together.

Jesus stood up and spoke to the wind and the waves, the way you might speak to a dog that’s barking: “Peace! Be still!” And they obeyed. Everything went completely calm.

The friends were amazed and a bit frightened. Only God can tell the sea what to do. So who was this man asleep in their boat?

What matters more to you: that Jesus can stop storms, or that he was in the boat with them all along?

There’s no wrong answer.

Jesus doesn’t always make our storms stop straight away. But he is stronger than any of them, and he doesn’t leave the boat.

Something to carry today

Jesus is stronger than my storm, and he is in the boat with me.

If you’d like to respond

  1. Borrow Jesus’ words as a prayer when you feel stormy inside:

    Jesus, please speak your “Peace! Be still!” to my storm.

  2. Use your own words and tell him what your storm is.
  3. Breathe slowly three times, and imagine the water going calm.
For the grown-up

What this shows about Jesus: he has God’s own authority over creation (compare Psalm 107:28–29), and he is present with his frightened friends.

Listening well: if your child names a real storm, such as illness, divorce or grief, don’t promise that it will stop. Say what is true: Jesus is with them, and so are you.
