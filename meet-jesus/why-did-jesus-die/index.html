Meet Jesus · Reading 7 of 8

Why did Jesus die?

  • John 10:11–15
  • About 3 minutes aloud
  • For children of every age

Read this with a grown-up, or read it yourself. When you see a pause, stop and think for a moment.

A moment you might knowSomeone who stays

Think of someone who looks after you. When you are ill in the night, who comes? When something goes wrong, who stays with you instead of walking away?

Pause. Who did you think of?

Jesus once described himself using a picture of someone who stays.

From the BibleThe good shepherd

Retelling

In Jesus’ time, shepherds stayed out with their sheep day and night. They knew each one. When wolves came, a good shepherd stood between the wolf and the sheep.

Bible text — Jesus is speaking

11“I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep. 12He who is a hired hand, and not a shepherd, who doesn’t own the sheep, sees the wolf coming, leaves the sheep, and flees. The wolf snatches the sheep, and scatters them. 13The hired hand flees because he is a hired hand, and doesn’t care for the sheep. 14I am the good shepherd. I know my own, and I’m known by my own; 15even as the Father knows me, and I know the Father. I lay down my life for the sheep.”

John 10:11–15, World English Bible. Lays down his life means gives up his life. A hired hand is someone paid to do a job. Flees means runs away.

Wonder togetherHe didn’t run away

What’s the difference between the good shepherd and the hired hand?

Let them find it: one runs away, one stays and gives his life.

Jesus said this before he died. He knew what was coming, and he chose not to run away. The Bible says that when he died on the cross, he was doing what a good shepherd does: putting himself between his sheep and the thing that would destroy them.

The “wolf” in our lives is sin, and the death it brings: all the wrong things that spoil us and pull us away from God. We couldn’t fight it on our own. So Jesus did. He took the blame and the punishment for our wrongs, so that we could be forgiven and belong to God for ever. And then, three days later, he came back to life. The good shepherd is alive, and he still knows his sheep by name.

Jesus said, “I know my own.” How does it feel to be known by someone who would give everything for you?

Leave a long pause here.

Something to carry today

Jesus died because he loves me. He is the shepherd who stayed.

If you’d like to respond

  1. Borrow these words:

    Jesus, thank you for not running away. Thank you for giving your life for me. I want to be one of your sheep.

  2. Use your own words. Tell Jesus what you think about what he did.
  3. Be quiet and just say “thank you” in your heart.

If you’d like to talk more about following Jesus, ask a grown-up you trust. It’s a good question to bring to church too.

For the grown-up

What this shows about Jesus: his death wasn’t an accident or a defeat. He chose to give his life out of love for his sheep (see also John 10:17–18, Mark 10:45, 1 Peter 2:24–25).

Listening well: children may ask, “Why couldn’t God just forgive?” or “Did it hurt?” Answer simply and truthfully. Yes, it hurt. Wrong things are serious, and Jesus loved us enough to deal with them himself. You don’t need to describe the crucifixion in detail.

If your child wants to respond to Jesus: let them take the lead and keep it simple. There’s no special formula. Talk with your church about next steps.

For younger children: focus on the picture: the shepherd stays, the shepherd knows each sheep’s name, and the shepherd is alive.
