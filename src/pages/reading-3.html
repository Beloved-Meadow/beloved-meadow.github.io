Meet Jesus · Reading 3 of 12

Can Jesus still love me when I’ve done something wrong?

  • Luke 19:1–10
  • About 3 minutes aloud
  • For children of every age

Read this with a grown-up, or read it yourself. When you see a pause, stop and think for a moment.

A moment you might knowThat heavy feeling

You’ve done something wrong. Maybe you broke something and hid the pieces. Maybe you said something mean, and you saw your friend’s face change.

Now there’s a heavy, hot feeling in your tummy. Part of you wants to hide. Part of you wants to say, “It wasn’t me.”

Today’s story is about a man who had done a lot of wrong things, and what happened when Jesus found him.

From the BibleThe man in the tree

Retelling

Zacchaeus lived in a town called Jericho. His job was collecting taxes, and he was in charge of other tax collectors. Tax collectors often took more money than they should and kept the extra. People in Jericho knew it, and they didn’t like him.

Bible text

1He entered and was passing through Jericho. 2There was a man named Zacchaeus. He was a chief tax collector, and he was rich. 3He was trying to see who Jesus was, and couldn’t because of the crowd, because he was short. 4He ran on ahead, and climbed up into a sycamore tree to see him, for he was going to pass that way. 5When Jesus came to the place, he looked up and saw him, and said to him, “Zacchaeus, hurry and come down, for today I must stay at your house.” 6He hurried, came down, and received him joyfully. 7When they saw it, they all murmured, saying, “He has gone in to lodge with a man who is a sinner.”

8Zacchaeus stood and said to the Lord, “Behold, Lord, half of my goods I give to the poor. If I have wrongfully exacted anything of anyone, I restore four times as much.” 9Jesus said to him, “Today, salvation has come to this house, because he also is a son of Abraham. 10For the Son of Man came to seek and to save that which was lost.”

Luke 19:1–10, World English Bible. Murmured means grumbled. Wrongfully exacted means took unfairly. Restore means give back.

Wonder togetherWho did the looking?

Imagine you’re Zacchaeus, up in the tree. Jesus stops right underneath you, looks up and says your name. What would you feel?

Pause. Close your eyes and picture it if you like.

Look carefully at what Jesus does first. He doesn’t tell Zacchaeus off. He doesn’t say, “Give the money back, and then I’ll come.” He stops, looks up, calls him by name and goes to his house. Everyone grumbles, but Jesus goes anyway.

Now look at what happens next. Nobody tells Zacchaeus to give the money back. He stands up and decides to, all by himself, and gives back four times as much.

Why do you think being with Jesus made Zacchaeus want to put things right?

Pause. Let everyone have a go.

When you think you’ll be pushed away for what you did, hiding feels safest. But when you know you are loved, you can stop hiding. You can tell the truth.

Being forgiven doesn’t mean the wrong thing didn’t matter. It did. It mattered so much that Jesus went to the cross to carry it for us. Because of that, we can say, “I did it. I’m sorry.” And then, like Zacchaeus, we can put right what we can.

Jesus said he came “to seek and to save that which was lost.” Zacchaeus thought he was the one looking for Jesus. But Jesus was looking for him.

Something to carry today

Jesus comes looking for me, even when I’ve done wrong. With him, I can tell the truth.

If you’d like to respond

You can choose. You might:

  1. Borrow these words, quietly or aloud:

    Jesus, I did something wrong. I’m sorry. Thank you that you still love me. Please help me put it right.

  2. Use your own words. You can tell God exactly what happened. He already knows, and he isn’t going anywhere.
  3. Pray silently. This can stay between you and God.

If there’s something you need to put right, like saying sorry, giving something back or telling the truth, a grown-up you trust can help you work out how.

For the grown-up

What this shows about Jesus: Jesus goes towards people who have done wrong. His welcome comes first, and Zacchaeus changes because of it, not to earn it.

Listening well: resist turning this into “so be good like Zacchaeus”. The story is about Jesus’ welcome. If your child tells you something they have done, thank them for telling the truth before you deal with what happens next. That moment is a small picture of the gospel.

Putting things right: help your child think of a step that fits what happened, and keep it in proportion. Zacchaeus’s generous response was his own choice.

Take care: sometimes a child talks about “something wrong” that was actually done to them. If that happens, make it clear that it’s not their fault, and follow your safeguarding procedures or contact the appropriate local services.
