Meet Jesus · Reading 2 of 12

Does Jesus have room for me?

  • Mark 10:13–16
  • About 3 minutes aloud
  • For children of every age

Read this with a grown-up, or read it yourself. When you see a pause, stop and think for a moment.

A moment you might know“Sorry, it’s full”

It’s break time. A group of children are playing a game you love. You walk over and ask, “Can I play?”

Someone says, “No. It’s full.” Or nobody answers at all. They just carry on playing, as if you’re not there.

You stand at the edge for a moment. Then you walk away.

Pause. Has something like that ever happened to you? You don’t have to say.

From the BibleThe day the children were sent away

Retelling

Wherever Jesus went, crowds followed him. One day, some people brought their children to him. They wanted Jesus to touch them and bless them. But Jesus’ closest friends, the disciples, stood in the way and told them off. Maybe they thought Jesus was too busy. Maybe they thought children didn’t matter very much.

Bible text

13They were bringing to him little children, that he should touch them, but the disciples rebuked those who were bringing them. 14But when Jesus saw it, he was moved with indignation, and said to them, “Allow the little children to come to me! Don’t forbid them, for God’s Kingdom belongs to such as these. 15Most certainly I tell you, whoever will not receive God’s Kingdom like a little child, he will in no way enter into it.” 16He took them in his arms, and blessed them, laying his hands on them.

Mark 10:13–16, World English Bible. Rebuked means told off. Moved with indignation means Jesus was cross because something was unfair.

Wonder togetherWatch what Jesus does

Let’s go back to the end of the story. Jesus took the children in his arms. He put his hands on them. He blessed them.

What do you think the children learned about Jesus from what he did?

Wait here for as long as it takes. Notice something before anyone explains.

Here are some things other people have noticed. Did you spot any of them?

  • Jesus was cross. He wasn’t cross with the children, though. He was cross with the people who were sending them away.
  • He didn’t just let the children stay. He called them to come to him.
  • He didn’t give them a quick wave. He picked them up and held them.
  • He said God’s kingdom belongs to children like them. They weren’t waiting at the edge. They were right in the middle.

When the disciples said, “There’s no room,” Jesus said, “Yes, there is.” He still says that today, to you.

Jesus said grown-ups need to receive God’s kingdom “like a little child”. What do you think children know how to do that grown-ups sometimes forget?

Pause. Any answer is worth hearing.

Something to carry today

Jesus makes room for me. I don’t have to wait until I’m older or better.

If you’d like to respond

You can choose. You might:

  1. Borrow these words:

    Jesus, thank you that you have room for me. Please be close to anyone who feels left out today.

  2. Use your own words. If you’ve been left out, you can tell Jesus how it felt. He won’t say it doesn’t matter.
  3. Be still for a moment and imagine Jesus calling you over.

If you’d like something to try: tomorrow, keep an eye out for anyone standing at the edge. You might be the one who says, “Come and join in.”

For the grown-up

What this shows about Jesus: he doesn’t put up with children, he welcomes them. He gets cross when they’re pushed away, and he holds them close.

Listening well: leave the first question open for a long time. Children often notice something an adult would miss, like the fact that Jesus was angry, or that he used his hands. Name what they noticed back to them: “You saw that he picked them up. Why do you think he did that?”

If your child shares a story of being left out, listen to that first. Don’t rush to fix it or to the lesson. “That sounds really lonely” is often enough. The idea to try at the end is optional, so let it come from comfort, not pressure.

About verse 15: you might wonder together what it means to receive something “like a little child”, which is with open hands, as a gift and without trying to earn it.
