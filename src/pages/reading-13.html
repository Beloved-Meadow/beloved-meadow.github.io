Meet Jesus · Reading 13 of 13

What if I can’t get to Jesus on my own?

  • Mark 2:1–12
  • About 3 minutes aloud
  • For children of every age

Read this with a grown-up, or read it yourself. When you see a pause, stop and think for a moment.

A moment you might knowToo small to reach

Have you ever been at the back of a crowd, with everyone taller than you, and you couldn’t see a thing? Or felt too tired, too shy or too worried to go and ask for something yourself? It’s a lonely feeling when the thing you need is on the other side of everyone else.

Pause. Who helps you when you can’t manage by yourself?

From the BibleFour friends and a hole in the roof

Retelling

Jesus was in a house in Capernaum, and word got round. Soon the house was so full that people were crowded all the way out of the door. Jesus was telling them God’s message. Then something happened that nobody expected.

Bible text

3Four people came, carrying a paralytic to him. 4When they could not come near to him for the crowd, they removed the roof where he was. When they had broken it up, they let down the mat that the paralytic was lying on. 5Jesus, seeing their faith, said to the paralytic, “Son, your sins are forgiven you.”

Mark 2:3–5, World English Bible. A paralytic is someone who can’t walk or move their body as they would like. Sins are the wrong things we do, which spoil things between us and God.
Retelling

Some teachers of the law were sitting nearby, and they were shocked. They said to themselves that only God can forgive sins. Jesus knew what they were thinking. He said he wanted them to know that he has authority on earth to forgive sins, and then he turned back to the man on the mat.

Bible text — Jesus is speaking

11“I tell you, arise, take up your mat, and go to your house.” 12He arose, and immediately took up the mat, and went out in front of them all; so that they were all amazed, and glorified God, saying, “We never saw anything like this!”

Mark 2:11–12, World English Bible. To glorify God means to say how great he is.

Wonder togetherCarried to Jesus

What did the four friends do when they couldn’t get through the door?

Pause. Picture the man coming down through the roof. How might he feel?

The man couldn’t walk, and the crowd was in the way. But his friends kept going until he was right in front of Jesus. The Bible doesn’t say a word about the broken roof. It says Jesus saw their faith, and he called the man “Son”.

What did Jesus say to the man first, before anything else?

Let them find it in verse 5. It may surprise them.

Jesus said, “Your sins are forgiven you.” Forgiveness was the first thing he gave. Then, to show that he really can forgive, Jesus told the man to get up. And he did! He picked up his mat and walked out in front of everyone. Jesus can do what only God can do.

And here’s good news: you don’t need a roof to reach Jesus. You can talk to him anywhere, even in a whisper. And when that feels hard, other people can carry you to him by praying for you.

Something to carry today

Jesus welcomes the people who are brought to him, and he forgives.

If you’d like to respond

You can choose. You might:

  1. Borrow these words:

    Jesus, thank you that you forgive. Thank you for the people who bring me to you.

  2. Use your own words. You could tell Jesus about someone you’d like to carry to him in prayer.
  3. Be quiet for a moment and picture Jesus looking up at you through the hole in the roof, glad to see you.
For the grown-up

What this shows about Jesus: he welcomes people who are brought to him, he sees the faith of the friends, and he deals with our deepest need first: forgiveness. By forgiving sins and then healing, he shows he has the authority that belongs to God (compare Mark 2:7, 10).

Listening well: children often notice the roof first, and that’s fine. Let them enjoy it. Then wait to see if anyone notices what Jesus said first. A child who feels they can’t pray, or who has a friend who is ill, may find this passage moving.

Care: the passage doesn’t say the man’s illness was caused by his own wrongdoing, and neither should we. In John 9:1–3, Jesus refuses to blame a man’s blindness on his own sin or his parents’. Be careful not to promise that everyone we pray for will be healed. Jesus is kind and has authority, and we can bring people to him honestly, trusting him with the outcome.

If your child, or someone they love, lives with a disability or a long illness, notice together that Jesus welcomed the man and called him “Son” before anything else changed. The man mattered to Jesus already.

For older children: read the whole story in Mark 2:1–12. Why were the teachers upset? What did Jesus want them to see?

You’ve reached the end of the readings. A good next step is to read Mark’s Gospel together, a few verses a night.
