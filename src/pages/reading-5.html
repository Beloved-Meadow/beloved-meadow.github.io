Meet Jesus · Reading 5 of 12

Can I ask Jesus for what I need?

  • Mark 10:46–52
  • About 3 minutes aloud
  • For children of every age

Read this with a grown-up, or read it yourself. When you see a pause, stop and think for a moment.

A moment you might know“Shh, not now”

You need to ask something. It matters to you. But the grown-ups are busy talking, and when you try to say it, someone says, “Shh. Not now.”

So you wait. And you wonder whether anyone will ever have time to listen.

Pause. Has that happened to you? How did it feel?

From the BibleThe man who wouldn’t be quiet

Retelling

Bartimaeus couldn’t see. Every day he sat beside the road out of the town of Jericho and begged for money. One day he heard a big crowd coming, and someone said, “It’s Jesus!”

Bible text

47When he heard that it was Jesus the Nazarene, he began to cry out, and say, “Jesus, you son of David, have mercy on me!” 48Many rebuked him, that he should be quiet, but he cried out much more, “You son of David, have mercy on me!” 49Jesus stood still, and said, “Call him.” They called the blind man, saying to him, “Cheer up! Get up. He is calling you!”

50He, casting away his cloak, sprang up, and came to Jesus. 51Jesus asked him, “What do you want me to do for you?” The blind man said to him, “Rabboni, that I may see again.” 52Jesus said to him, “Go your way. Your faith has made you well.” Immediately he received his sight, and followed Jesus on the way.

Mark 10:47–52, World English Bible. Have mercy on me means “please be kind to me and help me”. Rabboni means “my teacher”.

Wonder togetherJesus stood still

The crowd said, “Be quiet!” What did Jesus do?

Let them find it in the story.

A whole crowd was walking with Jesus. He was on his way somewhere important. But when he heard one voice shouting for help, he stood still. He told the people who had been saying “Shh!” to go and fetch Bartimaeus.

Then Jesus asked a surprising question: “What do you want me to do for you?” Of course Jesus could see that Bartimaeus was blind. But he wanted to hear Bartimaeus say it in his own words.

If Jesus stood still in front of you and asked, “What do you want me to do for you?”, what would you say?

You can answer out loud or just in your head.

Jesus doesn’t always give us exactly what we ask for. Sometimes he gives something better, and sometimes we have to wait. But he never tells us to be quiet. You can always ask him.

Something to carry today

Jesus stops for me. I can tell him what I need.

If you’d like to respond

You can choose. You might:

  1. Borrow Bartimaeus’s words and then add your own:

    Jesus, please be kind to me and help me. What I need is ____.

  2. Use your own words. Big things and small things are both allowed.
  3. Pray silently. Jesus hears you just as clearly.
For the grown-up

What this shows about Jesus: he stops for the person everyone else wants to keep quiet, and he invites them to say what they need.

Listening well: if your child asks for something big, like healing for someone or for a family problem to end, don’t promise the outcome. You can say, “Let’s ask Jesus together, and keep telling him.”

Notice the ending: Bartimaeus “followed Jesus on the way”. Older children might wonder what changed for him besides his eyes.

For younger children: act it out. One person shouts “Jesus, help me!”, everyone else says “Shh!”, and then the “Jesus” stands still and calls.
