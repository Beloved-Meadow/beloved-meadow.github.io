Meet Jesus · Reading 4 of 12

Can I talk to God when I’m scared?

  • Mark 14:32–36
  • About 3 minutes aloud
  • For children of every age

Read this with a grown-up, or read it yourself. When you see a pause, stop and think for a moment.

A moment you might knowWhen your heart beats fast

It’s dark, and the house makes a noise you don’t recognise. Or it’s the night before something hard, like a test, a hospital visit or a first day somewhere new. Your heart beats fast. Your thoughts go round and round.

What do you usually do when you feel scared?

Pause. Every answer is allowed.

From the BibleJesus in the garden at night

Retelling

This happened on the night before Jesus died. He knew that soldiers were coming to arrest him and that he was going to suffer. After supper, he went with his friends to an olive garden called Gethsemane.

Bible text

32They came to a place which was named Gethsemane. He said to his disciples, “Sit here, while I pray.” 33He took with him Peter, James, and John, and began to be greatly troubled and distressed. 34He said to them, “My soul is exceedingly sorrowful, even to death. Stay here, and watch.”

35He went forward a little, and fell on the ground, and prayed that, if it were possible, the hour might pass away from him. 36He said, “Abba, Father, all things are possible to you. Please remove this cup from me. However, not what I desire, but what you desire.”

Mark 14:32–36, World English Bible. Abba is a close, family word for “father”. This cup means the suffering ahead of him.

Wonder togetherJesus told the truth about how he felt

Which words in the story tell us how Jesus was feeling?

Pause. Look back at the Bible text if you like.

Greatly troubled. Distressed. Exceedingly sorrowful. He fell on the ground. Jesus, God’s own Son, was frightened and very sad. He didn’t pretend he was fine.

Then look at what he did with those feelings. He talked to his Father. He called him Abba. He said exactly what he wanted: “Please remove this cup from me.” And then he trusted his Father with what would happen.

God didn’t take that hard night away. The next day, Jesus died on the cross for us. But he wasn’t abandoned, and it wasn’t the end. Three days later, God raised him to life. Jesus knows what the hardest kind of fear feels like, so he understands yours.

If you could tell God about one thing that worries you, what would it be?

You can think it, say it or keep it to yourself.

Something to carry today

I can tell God exactly how I feel, even when I’m scared. Jesus did.

If you’d like to respond

You can choose. Here are some words you could borrow. Use one line or all of them:

Father, I’m scared.
Please be with me.
Please help me.
I don’t understand everything, but I’m trusting you.

You don’t have to say the last line until you mean it. Jesus told his Father what he really wanted first.

Or use your own words, as short as you like. “Help” is a real prayer.

Or pray quietly, without any words at all. God hears that too.

You don’t have to be scared alone. Jesus took his friends with him into the garden. Talking to God doesn’t mean you can’t talk to people too. Tell a grown-up you trust, like a parent, a teacher or a leader at church.

If someone is hurting you, or you don’t feel safe, tell a grown-up you trust straight away. You won’t be in trouble for telling.

For the grown-up

What this shows about Jesus: Jesus prayed honestly when he was in anguish, and he trusted his Father. He shows us that fear and faith can be in the same prayer.

Listening well: if your child names a fear, take it seriously before you reassure them. “That sounds really scary. Thank you for telling me.” Don’t promise outcomes that God hasn’t promised. It’s true to say that God is with them and hears them.

Grief and big fears: if a fear involves illness, death or a loss in the family, it’s fine to say, “I don’t know everything, but I know Jesus understands, and I’m here.” You can come back to the conversation another day.

When to get help: if fears keep your child from sleeping, eating or going about their day, talk to your GP or school. If anything your child says makes you worried about their safety, follow safeguarding procedures or contact local services.
