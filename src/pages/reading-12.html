Where is Jesus now?

  • John 14:1–3
  • About 3 minutes aloud
  • For children of every age

Read this with a grown-up, or read it yourself. When you see a pause, stop and think for a moment.

A moment you might knowGetting a room ready

Has someone ever got a room ready for you, like a grandparent making up a bed, putting out a towel or leaving something special on the pillow? It feels good to arrive somewhere and know someone was expecting you.

Pause. Where do you feel most at home?

From the BibleA promise on a sad night

Retelling

On the night before he died, Jesus told his friends he would soon be going away. They were upset and confused. So Jesus gave them a promise.

Bible text — Jesus is speaking

1“Don’t let your heart be troubled. Believe in God. Believe also in me. 2In my Father’s house are many homes. If it weren’t so, I would have told you. I am going to prepare a place for you. 3If I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and will receive you to myself; that where I am, you may be there also.”

John 14:1–3, World English Bible. Troubled means worried and upset. Prepare means get ready.

Wonder togetherA place for you

What did Jesus say he was going to do?

Let them find all three: go, prepare a place, come again.

After Jesus rose from the dead, he went back to his Father. Christians call this the ascension (Acts 1:9–11). So where is Jesus now? He is alive, with his Father. And he is getting a place ready for everyone who belongs to him. One day he will come again.

Jesus also said, “If it weren’t so, I would have told you.” He was being honest with his friends. He doesn’t make promises he can’t keep.

What do you think it will be like to be where Jesus is?

Imagine together. Wonder is welcome here.

Something to carry today

Jesus is alive with his Father, getting a place ready for me.

If you’d like to respond

  1. Borrow these words:

    Jesus, thank you for getting a place ready for me. Help my heart not to be troubled.

  2. Use your own words. If you miss someone who has died, you can tell Jesus about them.
  3. Be quiet and imagine arriving at a home where you are expected.
For the grown-up

What this shows about Jesus: he is honest, he goes ahead of us, and he promises to bring his people to be with him.

Grief: this passage is often read at funerals. If your child is grieving, let them lead. Don’t make confident claims about a particular person’s eternal state. You can say, “Jesus is kind and good, and we can trust him with the people we love.”

For older children: read on to John 14:6. How is Jesus “the way”?

You’ve reached the end of the readings. A good next step is to read Mark’s Gospel together, a few verses a night.
