Meet Jesus · Reading 6 of 12

What if I let Jesus down?

  • Luke 22:60–62 · John 21:15–17
  • About 3 minutes aloud
  • For children of every age

Read this with a grown-up, or read it yourself. When you see a pause, stop and think for a moment.

A moment you might know“I promise!”

Have you ever promised something and then not done it? Maybe you said, “I’ll always stick up for you,” and then, when it was hard, you didn’t.

Afterwards, it’s hard to look that person in the eye. You wonder if they’ll ever trust you again.

From the BiblePeter’s worst night and best morning

Retelling

Peter was one of Jesus’ closest friends. He promised that he would never leave Jesus, even if it meant dying with him. But on the night Jesus was arrested, people around a fire asked Peter, “Aren’t you one of his friends?” Peter was frightened. Three times he said, “I don’t know him.”

Bible text

60…Immediately, while he was still speaking, a rooster crowed. 61The Lord turned, and looked at Peter. Then Peter remembered the Lord’s word, how he said to him, “Before the rooster crows you will deny me three times.” 62He went out, and wept bitterly.

Luke 22:60–62, World English Bible. Deny means say you don’t know someone.

Pause. How do you think Peter felt that night?

Retelling

Jesus died. Then he rose again. Some time later, Peter and his friends went fishing. Early in the morning, Jesus was on the beach with a fire, cooking breakfast for them.

Bible text

15So when they had eaten their breakfast, Jesus said to Simon Peter, “Simon, son of Jonah, do you love me more than these?” He said to him, “Yes, Lord; you know that I have affection for you.” He said to him, “Feed my lambs.” 16He said to him again a second time, “Simon, son of Jonah, do you love me?” He said to him, “Yes, Lord; you know that I have affection for you.” He said to him, “Tend my sheep.” 17He said to him the third time, “Simon, son of Jonah, do you have affection for me?” Peter was grieved because he asked him the third time, “Do you have affection for me?” He said to him, “Lord, you know everything. You know that I have affection for you.” Jesus said to him, “Feed my sheep.”

John 21:15–17, World English Bible. Affection means love. Tend means look after.

Wonder togetherThree times

Peter said “I don’t know him” three times. How many times did Jesus ask, “Do you love me?”

Count them together.

Three times. For every time Peter let Jesus down, Jesus gave him a chance to say “I love you.” Jesus didn’t pretend it hadn’t happened. But he didn’t throw Peter away either. He made him breakfast. Then he gave him an important job: look after my sheep, the people who follow me.

Why do you think Jesus gave Peter a job straight away?

There are lots of possible answers.

Something to carry today

When I let Jesus down, he doesn’t give up on me. He invites me back.

If you’d like to respond

  1. Borrow Peter’s words:

    Lord, you know everything. You know that I love you, even when I get it wrong.

  2. Use your own words. Tell Jesus about a time you let someone down.
  3. Stay quiet and imagine sitting by that breakfast fire with Jesus.
For the grown-up

What this shows about Jesus: his forgiveness restores people, not just forgives them. Peter goes from failure to a job of trust.

Listening well: children who are hard on themselves may say, “But I keep doing it.” Peter is a good friend for them, because he got things wrong often. Jesus still chose him.

For older children: notice how Jesus looked at Peter after the rooster crowed (Luke 22:61). What kind of look do you think it was?
