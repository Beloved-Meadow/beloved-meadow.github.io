Meet Jesus · Reading 8 of 12

Is Jesus really alive?

  • John 20:24–29
  • About 3 minutes aloud
  • For children of every age

Read this with a grown-up, or read it yourself. When you see a pause, stop and think for a moment.

A moment you might know“I’ll believe it when I see it”

Your friend says, “I saw a fox in the park! A real one!” And you think, “Hmm. I’ll believe it when I see it.”

It’s normal to want to see things for ourselves, especially big, surprising things.

From the BibleThomas wasn’t there

Retelling

On the first Easter evening, Jesus came to his friends after he rose from the dead. But one of them, Thomas, was out. When he came back, everyone was buzzing: “We’ve seen Jesus!”

Bible text

25…But he said to them, “Unless I see in his hands the print of the nails, put my finger into the print of the nails, and put my hand into his side, I will not believe.” 26After eight days again his disciples were inside, and Thomas was with them. Jesus came, the doors being locked, and stood in the middle, and said, “Peace be to you.” 27Then he said to Thomas, “Reach here your finger, and see my hands. Reach here your hand, and put it into my side. Don’t be unbelieving, but believing.” 28Thomas answered him, “My Lord and my God!” 29Jesus said to him, “Because you have seen me, you have believed. Blessed are those who have not seen, and have believed.”

John 20:25–29, World English Bible. Blessed means truly happy and close to God.

Wonder togetherJesus came back for Thomas

Thomas had doubts. Did Jesus tell him off, or did he do something else?

Look carefully at verse 27.

Jesus came back, and this time Thomas was there. Jesus said “Peace” and offered Thomas exactly what he had asked for: “See my hands.” He didn’t make Thomas feel silly for doubting. He came close and helped him believe.

Then Thomas said something huge: “My Lord and my God!” He realised that Jesus wasn’t just alive. Jesus was God.

Jesus said, “Blessed are those who have not seen, and have believed.” Who do you think he was talking about?

Give them time. It includes us.

We can’t touch Jesus’ hands like Thomas did. But Thomas and the other friends wrote down what they saw, so that we could know too (John 20:31). And Jesus still meets people with doubts. He doesn’t push them away. He comes close.

Something to carry today

Jesus is alive. I can bring him my questions and my doubts.

If you’d like to respond

  1. Borrow Thomas’s words, if you’re ready to:

    My Lord and my God!

  2. Or use honest words:

    Jesus, sometimes I find it hard to believe. Please help me know you’re real.

  3. Or stay quiet and keep wondering. That’s allowed.
For the grown-up

What this shows about Jesus: he is risen, and he patiently meets people who doubt.

Listening well: if your child says, “I’m not sure I believe it,” thank them for being honest. You can share why you believe, and that it’s fine to keep asking. Doubts that are spoken can be explored. Doubts that are hidden tend to grow quietly.

For older children: 1 Corinthians 15:3–8 lists people who saw the risen Jesus, including more than five hundred at once. Why might Paul mention that many of them were still alive when he wrote?
