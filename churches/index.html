For Churches · Sunday school session · All children

Who is this? Jesus calms the storm

  • Mark 4:35–41
  • 15 minutes
  • Any group size
  • Adaptable for every age

A complete session you can lead from this page or a printout. It includes the words to say, a discussion with room for honest answers and an activity that only needs paper and pencils.

The big idea: the disciples asked, “Who then is this?” The storm showed them that Jesus has power over wind and waves, and that he was in the boat with them all along.

Before you start

  • You’ll need: one sheet of A4 paper per child, pencils or crayons, and a Bible (or this page).
  • Read Mark 4:35–41 yourself once, and think of a time you were afraid. You won’t need to share it.
  • Practise the storm (step 2) once on your own. It takes 30 seconds.
  1. 0–2Welcome and opening question
  2. 2–6Bible: make the storm, then read the passage
  3. 6–10Discussion: three questions
  4. 10–14Activity: storm cards
  5. 14–15Close with a prayer

0–2 minutesWelcome

Do: sit in a circle if you can, at the children’s level.

Hello, everyone. Put your hand up if you’ve ever been in a really big storm, with loud thunder, or wind that rattles the windows.

What did it feel like?

Do: take two or three answers. Thank each child. Then move on.

Today we’re going to hear about a storm so big that grown-up fishermen thought they were going to die. And we’re going to find out what it taught them about Jesus.

2–6 minutesMake the storm, then read

First, we’re going to make a storm with our hands. Copy me.

Do: lead each sound for about five seconds, getting louder: rub palms together (wind), tap fingers on palms (rain), pat knees (heavy rain), stamp feet (thunder). Then, while the stamping is loudest, hold up both hands and stop. Wait in silence for three seconds.

Did you hear how quiet it went? Keep that silence in your mind. Now listen to what happened.

Bible text — read aloud slowly

35On that day, when evening had come, he said to them, “Let’s go over to the other side.” 36Leaving the multitude, they took him with them, even as he was, in the boat. Other small boats were also with him. 37A big wind storm arose, and the waves beat into the boat, so much that the boat was already filled. 38He himself was in the stern, asleep on the cushion, and they woke him up, and told him, “Teacher, don’t you care that we are dying?”

39He awoke, and rebuked the wind, and said to the sea, “Peace! Be still!” The wind ceased, and there was a great calm. 40He said to them, “Why are you so afraid? How is it that you have no faith?” 41They were greatly afraid, and said to one another, “Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?”

Mark 4:35–41, World English Bible. Stern is the back of the boat. Rebuked means spoke sternly to.

Do: when you read “Peace! Be still!”, hold up your hands the way you did after the storm sounds. Then pause.

6–10 minutesDiscussion

Do: ask one question at a time. Wait at least five seconds before you add anything. These questions have more than one good answer.

1. The disciples woke Jesus up and said, “Teacher, don’t you care that we are dying?” Why do you think they said that?

Listen for: they were terrified, and Jesus was asleep, so it looked as if he didn’t care. Say that it’s normal to feel like that, and that the disciples said it to Jesus’ face. They brought him their fear, even though it came out as a complaint.

2. At the end, the disciples ask, “Who then is this?” What had they just found out about Jesus?

Listen for: he can command the wind and the sea, so he has God’s power. You might add: in the Old Testament, only God rules the sea (for example Psalm 107:28–29). The disciples were starting to realise that Jesus is more than a teacher.

3. Here’s a tricky one. What do you think matters more: that Jesus stopped the storm, or that Jesus was in the boat with them?

Listen for: there’s no single right answer. Both are true. If a child says, “Sometimes God doesn’t stop the storm,” say they’re right that this can happen. Jesus doesn’t always take hard things away, but he doesn’t leave us alone in them. Then thank the child for the honest question.

10–14 minutesActivity: storm cards

Fold your paper in half like a card. On the front, draw a boat in a big storm, with huge waves and lots of rain. Don’t forget to put Jesus in the boat.

Now open your card. Inside, you can draw or write something that feels like a storm for you, something that worries you or scares you. This part is private. You don’t have to show anyone, not even me.

On the back, copy the words Jesus said: “Peace! Be still!”

Do: write “Peace! Be still! — Mark 4:39” where everyone can see it. Walk around and comment only on the front of the cards (“I can see Jesus in your boat”). Never open a card. Children take their cards home.

Simpler version: for younger children or tired groups, just draw the boat and copy the words.

14–15 minutesClose

Hold your card closed in your hands. I’m going to pray. You can pray along with me in your head, or just listen.

Jesus, you are stronger than any storm. Thank you that you are with us when we’re scared. Please help us bring you our worries, the way your disciples did. Amen.

Take your card home. When you feel worried this week, look at the front and remember who is in the boat.

Leader notes: care, inclusion and safeguarding
  • Storm sounds: some children are sensitive to noise. Keep it short, let anyone take part quietly and tell children they can cover their ears.
  • Children who don’t read or write fluently can do the whole activity by drawing. They can also ask a helper to write the words on the back.
  • Private means private. Don’t collect cards or ask what’s inside. If a child chooses to tell you something that suggests they may be at risk, follow your church’s safeguarding policy straight away.
  • Grief and real storms: some children have lived through real disasters, illness or loss. Don’t promise that God will always stop bad things. Focus on Jesus being with us.
  • Younger children (under 6): keep the storm sounds and Bible text, ask only question 2, and let them draw the boat without the private inside page.
  • Older children (11+): let them read the passage aloud, add “Why do you think Jesus was asleep?”, and give them a minute to write a one-line prayer inside their card.
  • If you have extra time, retell the story together, with children taking the parts of the disciples and Jesus.
